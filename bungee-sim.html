How it works

The jumper is released with its bottom level with the anchor. It free-falls until the cord goes taut, after the carabiner offset, the string, and the slack rubber. Then the bands pull back. The simulation steps Newton's second law forward every 0.5 ms:

M·a = M·g − n·f(D / meff)

Here D is the total rubber stretch, n is bands per group, and each band stretches D/meff. Knots leave part of every band unable to stretch, so a knotted chain of m groups acts like meff = 0.818·m groups. The per-band force f comes from MTS tensile tests (held-out validation R² = 0.978). The accelerometer reads the cord tension divided by the jumper's weight, about 0 g in free fall.

Not modeled: string stretch, wall contact, the jolt when the string snaps taut, and stretch-rate stiffening. Hysteresis only affects the rebounds, not the first (scored) drop. Fits are valid to 378 mm of stretch per band; the 8.3 N per-band cap comes from failure tests.
