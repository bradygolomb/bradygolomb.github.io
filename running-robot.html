Personal project · 2024

Running Form Robot

A camera rover that chases a runner down the road and tells them their stride rate while they're still running.

Role

Sole designer and builder

Origin

Personal project

Built with

Salvaged RC platform · GoPro · AI motion capture

Output

Real-time stride rate (SPM)

Why I built it

Stride rate is one of the most useful things a runner can know about their form, and one of the most annoying to measure well. Watches estimate it from wrist motion. Treadmill analysis puts you in a lab, on a belt, running in a way that isn't quite how you run outside. Filming yourself means someone else has to hold the camera, and they can't keep up.

I'm a Division I track athlete, so this is a problem I have personally. I wanted feedback from the way I actually run — outdoors, at real speed, over real distance — without needing a person to film it.

How it works

The platform is an old RC car, re-engineered to carry a GoPro on a mount stable enough that the footage is usable for analysis rather than just watchable. Starting from a salvaged chassis meant I could spend the effort on the tracking and analysis instead of on building a vehicle from scratch.

AI-powered motion capture software handles the tracking, keeping the runner framed as the rover follows them and processing the footage to extract stride events. From those it computes an average stride rate and reports it back in real time — the runner gets the number while they're still running, which is the only point at which they can do anything with it.

The interesting difficulty was that both the subject and the camera are moving. A tracking system that assumes a fixed vantage point doesn't survive being mounted on something that's accelerating, cornering, and hitting road irregularities.

What came of it

It tracked runners successfully across varying speeds and distances and returned accurate stride rate measurements, which is enough to show the idea works: real-time coaching feedback from a follow camera, outdoors, without a person operating it.

This was the project where mechanical and software engineering stopped feeling like separate disciplines to me. The mount design determined whether the vision system had anything usable to work with, and the tracking behavior determined what the vehicle needed to do. Neither half could be designed without the other.
