How it works

Liquid butane holds the bottle near its saturation pressure, set by water temperature through the Antoine equation. That pressure pushes water out the 21 mm neck. Every millisecond the model updates flow rate, thrust, drag, and the rocket's mass, then steps Newton's second law forward in x and y:

T = ṁ·vₑ + (p − pₐ)·Aₑ, ṁ = C_d·Aₑ·√(2ρ_w·Δp)

When the water runs out, only gravity and quadratic drag act until landing. Three coefficients were fit to launch data: nozzle discharge C_d = 0.379, drag C_D = 1.341, and butane efficiency α = 0.497 (the share of the measured butane that acts as useful propellant).

To solve a shot, the model sweeps a 50 × 50 grid of angle and butane, scores each flight on how far it misses the gap height and the target, and refines the best point with Nelder-Mead.

Not modeled: wind, launch-seal variation, and how butane efficiency changes with charge size. The model is most accurate near 12 g; it under-predicts thrust at small charges and over-predicts at large ones.
